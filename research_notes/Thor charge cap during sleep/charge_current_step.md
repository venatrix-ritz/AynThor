Research date: 2026-10-08. Scope: documented mechanisms, what the Thor's own firmware and the Armada kernel expose, and what evidence would tell the candidates apart. No cause is asserted as fact.

## Q1. What do Qualcomm/kernel docs, patches, AOSP docs and teardown/forum reports say about step-wise charging profiles (JEITA, CC to CV, fast-charge to taper thresholds) near 85-90 % for 4.4 V (LiHV) cells?

### Takeaway
Qualcomm's documented charge profiles are tables of voltage-based (or SOC-based) FCC steps plus JEITA temperature zones plus a float voltage, and the reference profiles for 4.4 V cells step the current down at cell-voltage thresholds (4.0 V and 4.2 V in the one 4.4 V example; 4.3 V in a 4.35 V example), not at a fixed SOC. On pmic_glink platforms that logic runs in ADSP firmware, and the Thor's firmware image contains the step-charge, JEITA, top-off and "cv mode" machinery, but its battery-specific step table is not in any file I could read. No source I found documents a specific 89 % step.

### Cited Findings
- Qualcomm battery-profile bindings express step charging as `qcom,step-chg-ranges` (cell-voltage windows each with an FCC), alongside `qcom,jeita-fcc-ranges` and `qcom,jeita-fv-ranges`; the smb2 binding says step charging and SW-JEITA are taken from those three battery-profile properties. `[V-src]` `refs/thor-android/android_kernel_ayn_qcs8550-devicetrees@9b2d195:bindings/power/supply/qcom/qpnp-smb2.txt#L197-L199`
- A Qualcomm reference profile for a 4.4 V, 3300 mAh cell sets `max-voltage-uv` 4.40 V, `fg-cc-cv-threshold-uv` 4.39 V, FCC max 8 A, and `step-chg-ranges` of 3.5-4.0 V at 8 A, 4.0-4.2 V at 6 A, 4.2-4.4 V at 4 A, flagged `ocv-based-step-chg`. `[V-src]` `refs/thor-android/android_kernel_ayn_qcs8550-devicetrees@9b2d195:qcom/qg-batterydata-atl466271_3300mAh.dtsi#L3-L26` (a reference pack for a different battery, not the Thor's 6000 mAh pack)
- A 4.35 V, 3600 mAh reference profile steps 5.4 A to 3.6 A at 3.8 V and to 2.5 A at 4.3 V (`step-chg-ranges`), CC-CV threshold 4.34 V. `[V-src]` `refs/thor-android/android_kernel_ayn_qcs8550-devicetrees@9b2d195:qcom/qg-batterydata-alium-3600mah.dtsi#L3-L21`
- A 4.4 V, 3650 mAh profile uses JEITA FCC/FV ranges only (no step table): 5.325 A at 20-45 C, 1.775 A at 45-55 C, FV 4.4 V falling to 4.05 V above 45 C, CC-CV threshold 4.39 V. `[V-src]` `refs/thor-android/android_kernel_ayn_qcs8550-devicetrees@9b2d195:qcom/fg-gen4-batterydata-atl466274-3650mah.dtsi#L4-L22`
- The older HLOS-side Qualcomm implementation (`step-chg-jeita.c`, SMB5/SDM845 era) reads cell voltage (default hysteresis 100 mV), OCV (hysteresis 10 mV) or SOC (hysteresis 0) per profile flags, re-evaluates no more often than every 5 s, and votes the result as an FCC limit. `[V-src]` `OnePlusOSS/android_kernel_oneplus_sm8150@1dd473a:drivers/power/supply/qcom/step-chg-jeita.c#L84,L255-L282,L788-L790` (analog only: on SM8550 the equivalent runs in ADSP firmware, and this file is not that firmware)
- On pmic_glink platforms the kernel node `qcom,battery-charger` only talks to charger firmware on the remote DSP; its optional DT properties are thermal-mitigation FCC levels, wireless firmware name, shutdown voltage and display panels, with no profile tables. `[V-src]` `refs/thor-android/android_kernel_ayn_qcs8550-devicetrees@9b2d195:bindings/power/supply/qcom,battery-charger.txt#L4-L35` and `qcom/kalama.dtsi#L3532-L3541`
- The Thor's own ADSP config (`adsp_dtb.mbn`, the file the live Thor DTB names) contains `/sw/core/pmic/battman/platform_cfg` with `en-stepchg-jeita = 1`, `iterm-ma = 100`, `chg-done-timer-ms = 28080000`, `en-linear-soc = 1`, `soc-conv-pt = 500`, `min-linear-soc = 750`, and `chg_table_cfg` with `chg-table-max = 2` and three battery-ID resistor ranges (debug 2000-14000, normal 15000-140000, smart 240000-450000, units as written in the file). `[V-src]` `refs/upstream/armada@574da80:system_files/usr/lib/firmware/qcom/sm8550/ayn/thor/adsp_dtb.mbn` (FDT at file offset 208, decoded this session; node paths as above). ### Inferences
- `[UNV]` A step to a flat low current "within 20 s of a replug" matches the shape of a table lookup (voltage-, OCV- or SOC-keyed FCC stage, or a JEITA/thermal zone), which the firmware demonstrably has the code for; it does not by itself prove which key applies, because the Thor's step table is not readable here.
- `[UNV]` In the reference 4.4 V profile the voltage thresholds sit 0.2-0.4 V under the 4.4 V float. Ven's 4.296-4.301 V reading is in the same neighbourhood as a step threshold, but the Thor's actual thresholds are unknown, and Qualcomm's own 4.35 V example used 4.3 V, so a 4.3 V threshold is plausible, not documented for this cell.
- `[UNV]` Decoded JEITA values put a 36 C battery in the normal zone (10-45 C), so a JEITA soft-warm cut on battery temperature is unlikely if these config values are what the firmware really uses. The file's nominal values (4350 mV, 3000 mA) disagree with the sysfs 4.4 V `voltage_max` and with the 4.3 A observed, so this config may be only a reference-board default and the real limits may come from a table not present in the file (see Gaps).
- `[UNV]` `en-linear-soc` / `min-linear-soc 750` / `soc-conv-pt 500` suggest the reported SOC is linearized in the upper range, and the firmware has a CV-mode SOC filter. If so, "89 %" is a firmware-computed number tied to charge phase, and a step near 89 % may sit near the CV/top-off boundary. Key semantics are not documented in anything I read.

### Gaps
- Where `chg_table_cfg`'s two selectable tables (and the step windows `StepChgWinTotal`) live: not in `adsp_dtb.mbn`; possibly inside `adsp.mbn` or a separate battery-profile blob. Not found.
- No Qualcomm document stating a step at about 89 % for any 4.4 V pack. None found.
- LiHV-specific charging references of good quality: web search returned only generic Li-ion CC-CV pages (a search-assistant summary of 4.35/4.4 V behaviour was general knowledge, not a cited source, so it is not used).
- `qcom,step-chg-ranges` semantics for the SM8550 ADSP firmware (voltage vs OCV vs SOC) are not documented in anything I could read.

## Q2. Is a flat low current like this more consistent with the CV phase, a firmware charge-profile step, or a thermal/other limit? What does each predict, and what evidence would distinguish them?

### Takeaway
A flat current with a slowly rising voltage still well below the 4.4 V ceiling reads more like a current-regulated limit (firmware FCC stage, or an input-side limit) than textbook CV, but this is an inference from the observation alone; CV at a lowered float voltage and a thermal-mask limit are not excluded. The Thor's firmware writes the exact lines that would settle it (FCC/FV changes, JEITA zone, thermal mask, top-off state entry) into a log channel Armada already knows how to capture.

### Cited Findings
- `charge_type` in `qcom_battmgr` is the firmware's integer passed straight through (`BATT_CHG_TYPE` stored to `info.charge_type`, returned as is), so the kernel does not compute "Fast". `[V-src]` `torvalds/linux@37689bc:drivers/power/supply/qcom_battmgr.c#L53,L433,L535-L536,L1417-L1418`
- The `charge_type` ABI value list has Trickle, Fast, Standard, Adaptive, Custom, Long Life, Bypass; there is no "Taper" value, and "Long Life" is documented as "The charger reduces its charging rate in order to prolong the battery health." `[V-src]` `torvalds/linux@37689bc:Documentation/ABI/testing/sysfs-class-power#L376-L415`. Downstream msm kernels for pmic_glink devices use the same enum without a taper value. `[V-src]` `MotorolaMobilityLLC/kernel-msm@0450c50:include/linux/power_supply.h#L43-L53`
- Firmware-side state names relevant here (Fast_Charge, TopOff_Charge, Done, ReCharge, Not_Charging_Thermal) are present in the Thor `adsp.mbn`; the numbering 0-9 (0 ENTRY, 1 NO_CHG, 2 FAST, 3 TOP_OFF, 4 DONE, 5 RECHARGE, 6 TDONE, 7 NOT_CHARGING_THERMAL, 8 ERROR, 9 TEST) comes from a commenter reading an SM8750 (Odin 3) `.pdb`. `[V-src]` state names: `refs/upstream/armada@574da80:system_files/usr/lib/firmware/qcom/sm8550/ayn/thor/adsp.mbn` (0xc83695, 0xc83757). `[SEC]` numbering: `refs/_gh/issues.json` issue #402, final comment (different SoC; same state-machine family is `[UNV]`)
- The firmware aggregates several FCC sources: `vote_for_fcc: FCC set by HLOS is %d mA and FCC voted for thermal mitigation support is %d mA` (0xc749b7); `Failed to aggregate FCC/FV` (0xc71d9f); thermal-state change lines `Change to state %d because of thermal mask 0x0%x` (0xc7189e) and `Battery temp is not within charging limit ... (hysteresis)` (0xc731d0); periodic ADC line `vin_adc=%d mV, iin_adc=%d mA, skin_adc=%d C` (0xc7104f); `connector temp is %d C` (0xc74e92). `[V-src]` same `adsp.mbn`
- An AYN capacity-limit path exists in the firmware: `usb_charge_now=%d, limit_capacity_charge=%d, bat_capacity=%d curr_cap_state=%d last_cap_state=%d` (0xc7358f). `[V-src]` same file. The MgeeeeK fork's patch text describes it as property 16, an 80/100 flag. `[SEC]` `docs/armada/forks-and-related.md#L101` (summarizing the fork's patch `0921`; the fork author's on-device claim is unverified)
- The firmware's own log is exposed to Linux by `pmic_pdcharger_ulog` (built as a module in the Armada kernel) over the rpmsg channel `PMIC_LOGS_ADSP_APPS`, and Armada ships `armada-charge-debug [--watch SECONDS] [--ulog-only]` to capture it; the script warns never to `modprobe -r` the module (it blocks forever in GLINK teardown). `[V-src]` `refs/upstream/armada@574da80:packages/kernel/config/armada-kernel.config.overrides#L56`; `refs/upstream/armada@574da80:system_files/usr/bin/armada-charge-debug#L12-L20,L122-L156`
- That capture method has produced firmware log lines on another AYN device (Odin 3), including `Test mode` and `Cannot change state since current state is test`. `[SEC]` `refs/_gh/issues.json` issue #402, final comment
- Armada's userspace in the clone does not write `constant_charge_current`; the only battery-node writes found were in test fixtures. `[V-src]` `rg 'constant_charge_current|power_supply/battery|charge_type|input_current_limit'` over `refs/upstream/armada@574da80` excluding kernel patches (this session). Writers on the Thor are therefore this repo's tools (Gleipnir plugin, the old `thor-charge-limit` unit). `[V-src]` `docs/hardware/device-observed.md#L84,L89-L97`
- A field run on this Thor showed charge current can be input-limited: on a 250 W PD charger through a 100 W cable the contract was 8.5 V, `input_current_limit` 3 A and about 4.3 A reached the battery; on a charger that negotiated no PD contract, `status` read Charging while the battery drained. `[OBS]` `docs/hardware/device-observed.md#L94-L98`. In the Gleipnir test the node read 5460000 while `current_now` was 4.4 A at 72-73 %, i.e. current below the node value. `[OBS]` `docs/hardware/device-observed.md#L91`
- Two behaviours of the node were recorded: after a restore of its found value 9000000 the node read 5460000, and a write of 0 stopped the current within about 5 s. `[OBS]` `docs/hardware/device-observed.md#L90-L91`

### Inferences
- `[UNV]` Because the node moves by itself (3.8, 4.68, 5.46 A) and read 5.46 A right after a write of its max, it behaves like a firmware-effective FCC, not only the stored HLOS vote. If so, a reading of 9000000 during the plateau means the FCC aggregate was **not** the limiter at that moment, which would point at CV/top-off, an input-side limit, or a limit outside the FCC votes. If instead the node returns only the HLOS vote (the `vote_for_fcc` string suggests the effective value is an aggregate), 9000000 says nothing about firmware limits. These two readings cannot be separated from the data given.
- `[UNV]` Numeric observations, not explanations: remaining 11 % of 6.17 Ah is 0.68 Ah, which at 0.91 A is about 2690 s against the reported 2325 s (implied average about 1.05 A); 0.91 A is about 0.15C of 6.0-6.17 Ah; the node values 9.0, 5.46 and 4.68 A are 1.50C, 0.91C and 0.78C of 6.0 Ah (clean two-digit ratios, possibly FCC tables in C-rate terms, possibly coincidence).
- `[UNV]` Prediction table (what each candidate predicts, and the check that separates it):

| Candidate | Current | Voltage | Other predictions | Distinguishing evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CV / top-off at a float below 4.4 V | decays continuously (not flat) | pins at the float value, near-flat | firmware shows TopOff state or "cv mode"; FV in the "Config parameters changed" line is about 4.30 V | ulog `FV =` value and TopOff entry line; current trend vs V trend across minutes |
| CV at 4.4 V | decays smoothly | rises toward about 4.4 V | not consistent with V at 4.30 V and flat I (unless the 100 mV gap is path/measurement offset) | `voltage_now` approaching 4.39-4.40 V; ulog FV 4400 |
| Firmware step-charge stage (voltage/OCV/SOC keyed) | steps to a table value, flat within a stage | below the float, rises slowly (CC-like) | `voltage_now` shows a small dip about dI x R when current steps; step reappears at the same V/SOC on other replugs; hysteresis (no immediate return to high current) | ulog `StepChgJeitaParam ... Configured FCC=... FV=...`; same threshold on repeat at 85-90 % |
| JEITA zone / battery temperature | steps when temperature crosses a zone edge | below the float | tied to `temp` crossing 10/45/55 C (decoded config) | battery 36 C is mid-zone, so unlikely `[UNV]`; ulog `Initial Jeita Zone`, `JeitaZone/PrevJeitaZone` |
| Thermal mask (skin, connector, die, SMB) | steps/limits with a non-battery sensor | below the float | follows skin/connector/die temperature, which Linux may not report; fan state may matter | ulog `Change to state ... thermal mask`, `skin_adc`, `connector temp`; cool the device and re-test |
| Input-side limit (ICL/AICL/adapter) | battery current = input minus system load; may step after renegotiation | below the float | `qcom-battmgr-usb` `input_current_limit`, `current_now`, `voltage_now` change at the same moment; load-dependent | read the USB psy across the step; repeat with screen off/idle and a different PD source |
| HLOS vote (Gleipnir, old charge-limit unit, battery tools) | would show a node value below max | n/a | the node reads 9000000 (max), so unlikely | `journalctl -t gleipnir` around the replug; `systemctl status thor-charge-limit` |
| Battery-health / limit-capacity firmware flag | would stop or sharply cut above 80 % | n/a | current still flowing at 89 % argues against a hard 80 % cap; a "reduced-rate" mode is not documented for this firmware | ulog `limit_capacity_charge=` / `curr_cap_state` values (0xc7358f) |

- `[UNV]` The plain reading of the observation (flat current, V rising slowly, V 4.30 V, below both 4.35 V and 4.40 V) fits a current-regulated limit better than CV. That is a reading of the data, not a finding.
- `[UNV]` If a soft-warm compensation (300 mV) were applied to a 4350 mV float, the float would fall to about 4.05 V, below the 4.30 V seen, and charging would stop rather than hold at 0.91 A; this is an inference from the config values only, since the compensation semantics are not documented.

From existing `tweaks/battery-monitor.py` logs (1 s `fast` lines, 5 s `full` lines): `voltage_now` and `current_now` across the step; `internal_resistance` if exposed; `qcom-battmgr-usb` `input_current_limit`, `current_now`, `voltage_now`; `temp`; thermal zones (skin/connector/die if any). - Whether the Thor's on-device `adsp_dtb.mbn` / `adsp.mbn` equal the clone's (build `20261006.9c7dd3e` vs clone `574da80`): not compared. `refs/dts/thor_live.dts` is empty (0 bytes), so it could not be used for this.

## Q3. Does the qcom_battmgr driver or the Armada patches expose or set anything that controls or reports this (dynamic constant_charge_current, BATT_CHG_CTRL_LIM, charge_type)?

### Takeaway
Yes, in a limited way. Mainline `qcom_battmgr` defines `BATT_CHG_CTRL_LIM` (10) and `_MAX` (11) but does not use them; Armada's local patch `0903` wires them to `constant_charge_current` / `_max` (readable and writable), and `charge_type`, `voltage_max`, `time_to_full_avg` and `temp` are plain firmware passthroughs. Nothing in the kernel or Armada's userspace computes or sets a taper/step; the step is firmware behaviour the driver can only observe.

### Cited Findings
- Mainline defines `BATT_CHG_CTRL_LIM 10` and `BATT_CHG_CTRL_LIM_MAX 11` and never references them again (`grep -n BATT_CHG_CTRL_LIM` finds only lines 60-61); it maps `CHARGE_TYPE`, `VOLTAGE_MAX`, `TIME_TO_FULL_AVG`, `CHARGE_CONTROL_START/END_THRESHOLD` (25/26) into the sm8350 property map and returns whatever the firmware answers. `[V-src]` `torvalds/linux@37689bc:drivers/power/supply/qcom_battmgr.c#L50-L76,L429-L452,L1405-L1480`. Snapshot note: fetched from `master` on 2026-10-08; the latest commit touching the file is `37689bc` (2026-08-12, "fix battery chemistry strncmp length").
- Each sysfs read on an sm8350/sm8550-class battery issues a firmware `BATTMGR_BAT_PROPERTY_GET` for that one property, so a node can change between reads without any host write. `[V-src]` `torvalds/linux@37689bc:drivers/power/supply/qcom_battmgr.c#L455-L471` (`qcom_battmgr_bat_sm8350_update`) and `#L527`
- Armada patch `0903` (authored by Armada, "upstream: local"): adds `charge_current_limit` and `_max` fields; maps `CONSTANT_CHARGE_CURRENT` to `BATT_CHG_CTRL_LIM` and `_MAX` to `BATT_CHG_CTRL_LIM_MAX`; the response handler stores the firmware value into those fields (so a read returns the latest firmware answer); set sends `BATTMGR_BAT_PROPERTY_SET` for `BATT_CHG_CTRL_LIM`, rejects negative values, and updates the cache on success; commit text says writing 0 stops charging while the charger powers the system (bypass). `[V-src]` `refs/upstream/armada@574da80:packages/kernel/patches/0903-power-supply-qcom-battmgr-expose-the-charge-current-limit.patch#L2-L8,L24-L25,L33-L38,L46-L62,L78-L79,L96-L101`; provenance `refs/upstream/armada@574da80:packages/kernel/PATCHES.md#L397-L399`
- The other battmgr patches in Armada are `0900` (log USB adapter type), `0901` (set the mAh unit so CHARGE_FULL* appears), `0902` (map the firmware charge counter to CHARGE_NOW), `0503` (battery name). None sets FCC, taper or thresholds. `[V-src]` `refs/upstream/armada@574da80:packages/kernel/patches/series` and `packages/kernel/PATCHES.md#L386-L405`
- Downstream QTI driver precedent: `BATT_CHG_CTRL_LIM` is the property the kernel writes for thermal-mitigation FCC (`qcom,thermal-mitigation` levels, `thermal_levels[0] = BATT_CHG_CTRL_LIM_MAX`), mapped there to `CHARGE_CONTROL_LIMIT`, and re-applied after a remote-processor restart (`last_fcc_ua`). `[V-src]` `MotorolaMobilityLLC/kernel-msm@0450c50:drivers/power/supply/qti_battery_charger.c#L281-L282,L1123-L1145,L1405-L1413,L2087-L2145`. The ABI text for `charge_control_limit` is "Maximum allowable charging current. Used for charge rate throttling for thermal cooling or improving battery health." `[V-src]` `torvalds/linux@37689bc:Documentation/ABI/testing/sysfs-class-power#L331-L340`. `CONSTANT_CHARGE_CURRENT` is documented in the power-supply class doc as "constant charge current programmed by charger"; it has no entry in `sysfs-class-power`. `[V-src]` `torvalds/linux@37689bc:Documentation/power/power_supply_class.rst#L153-L157` (and grep of the ABI file)
- On the Thor, `charge_control_end_threshold` reads 0 and ignores writes; the MgeeeeK fork's patch text says GETs of properties 25/26 "always return 0" on the AYN firmware (so the 0 would be a firmware answer, not a kernel default). `[OBS]` `docs/hardware/device-observed.md#L75`; `[SEC]` `docs/armada/forks-and-related.md#L101`
- `time_to_full_avg` is property 19 (`BATT_TTF_AVG`) on sm8350/sm8550-class firmware; sc8280xp-class firmware uses a separate `BATTMGR_BAT_CHARGE_TIME` request. `[V-src]` `torvalds/linux@37689bc:drivers/power/supply/qcom_battmgr.c#L46,L69,L408,L446,L491-L498,L1460`. The Thor firmware has a TTF estimator keyed to the step scheme (`set_qbg_parameters_ttf_estimate ... StepChgScheme, StepChgWinTotal`, 0xc74f7e). `[V-src]` Thor `adsp.mbn`. Whether that estimator feeds property 19 is `[UNV]`.

### Inferences
- `[UNV]` The kernel side cannot be the source of a step to 0.91 A: no Armada patch computes FCC, and Armada userspace does not write the node. The firmware (plus any of this repo's own writers) is the only actor.
- `[UNV]` The Gleipnir test sequence (9000000, 6000000, 3000000, 0 in about 3 s) is itself stepwise, so a step shape alone does not identify a firmware profile; compare timing and values with Gleipnir's journal.

## Q4. Are there Thor/Armada/AYN reports of the same behaviour?

### Takeaway
I found no report of a mid-charge step to a flat current near 89 % in the Armada issue export or on the web. The Armada reports are about charging stuck at 4-9 W (wrong firmware or an unnegotiated PD contract), which is a different problem; one report mentions charge stopping at 85-94 % with the device off.

### Cited Findings
- Issue #61 (Thor): charging "often stays stuck in a 7W charging speed instead of kicking the 30/35W fast charging"; closed as fixed by Armada commit `738b52f`. `[V-src]` `refs/_gh/issues.json` #61
- Issue #89 (open, Thor Max, build 20260628): charging limited to about 4 W, 0 W when off; maintainer reply: ROCKNIX shipped a copy of the Odin 2 charger firmware on the Thor until early August, and the Thor got its own on 2026-08-02; issues #137, #143, #158, #189 were closed as duplicates of #89, and #149 was answered with "we have switched to using the specific firmwares for each device". `[V-src]` `refs/_gh/issues.json` #89, #137, #143, #149, #158, #189 (the repo's `adsp_dtb.mbn` files for Thor/Odin2/RP6 decode identically even now, per Q1)
- Issue #189 (Ayaneo Pocket S 2K): "the battery also refuses to fully charge, stopping somewhere around 85-94% when turned off and plugged in", fully charges only after booting Android. Not the same device or symptom. `[V-src]` `refs/_gh/issues.json` #189 (user report; cause not established)
- Issue #402 (Odin 3, SM8750; closed): the charger firmware stuck in "TEST MODE (state 9)" because `adsp_dtb.mbn` lacked battery-auth config; fixed by shipping the Armada `odin3` `adsp.mbn`/`adsp_dtb.mbn`. Source of the ulog-capture method and of the state numbering. `[SEC]` `refs/_gh/issues.json` #402
- Issue #363 (open): request for an 80 % charge limit on Odin 3/Thor. No current-step discussion. `[V-src]` `refs/_gh/issues.json` #363
- No issue or PR in the export matched "taper", "JEITA", "charge_type", "constant_charge", "TOP_OFF" or "89" in a charging-step sense, apart from #402 (state table) and #534/#550 (the SM8250 `pm8150b` charger float voltage; different SoC and driver). `[V-src]` Python scan of `refs/_gh/issues.json` and `prs.json` this session (342 issues, 258 PRs)
- Web search for Thor-specific "charging slows after 80/90 %" returned only generic phone pages. `[SEC]` search results 2026-10-08 (no Thor source)

### Inferences
- `[UNV]` Because the Armada tracker's charging complaints are about the 4-9 W floor and firmware mix-ups, the Thor's current step may simply be unreported rather than unusual; absence of a report is weak evidence.

### Gaps
- Discord/Reddit/AYN forum posts were not reachable through search; none cited.
- Freshness of the GitHub export: it is as of the last `scripts/fetch-gh-meta.ps1` run `[UNV]`; issue dates were not scanned.
